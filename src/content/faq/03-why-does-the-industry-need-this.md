---
question: "Why does an industry need open-source software at all? Isn't the commercial market working?"
section: "The basics"
order: 3
---
The commercial market works for some agencies and not for others. The AR software market has essentially split into two tiers: older platforms struggling to keep up with modern compliance, integration, and interface standards, and newer platforms whose per-seat or transaction-based pricing cuts deeply into profit margins. The middle ground, capable software at a price any agency can absorb, has been hollowed out.

Larger agencies are not exempt. Commercial pricing scales with size, so being big does not make the problem go away. Some larger agencies, frustrated with the cost and constraints of off-the-shelf systems, have invested in building their own. That may satisfy their needs, but the agency ends up operating, in effect, as a software company without the intent or infrastructure to do so, maintaining homegrown code that becomes expensive to support, hard to staff around, and difficult to modernize. Others stay on legacy commercial platforms whose vendors treat them as long-term revenue rather than partners, and dedicate significant internal engineering effort to working around the system rather than with it.

The result is an industry in which very few agencies, of any size, are well served by the status quo. An open-source alternative is built for that whole picture, not one slice of it. It puts modern software within reach of smaller agencies, offers mid-sized ones a path that does not require a leap to enterprise pricing, and gives larger agencies a shared, professionally stewarded codebase they can extend and contribute to instead of building their own from scratch or paying premium prices for a system they end up working around.
