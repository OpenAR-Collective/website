---
question: "How is this different from a free trial, a freemium tier, or \"community edition\" software?"
group: "The software"
section: "Open source, explained"
order: 16
---
Free trials and freemium products are commercial offerings designed to convert you into a paying customer. They typically have time limits, feature limits, user limits, or all three. The vendor's goal is to give you enough to get hooked and then charge you when you outgrow the free version. "Community editions" of commercial software are similar: usually a stripped-down version of a paid product, controlled by a company whose primary business is selling the full version.

Open-source software is structurally different. There is no paid version. There is no upsell. The Collective has no commercial product to sell you, and the Collective's nonprofit charter legally prohibits it from operating one. The Foundation exists, in part, as the legal steward of the software's permanently free nature: its incorporation documents, its nonprofit charter, and its governing board are the mechanisms that ensure the software remains free, in perpetuity, regardless of who is running the Foundation in the future. The software is the same software whether you are a one-person agency or a thousand-seat enterprise, and no one, including the founder, has the authority to suddenly start charging for it later. That permanence is the entire point.
