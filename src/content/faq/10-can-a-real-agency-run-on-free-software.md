---
question: "Can a real agency, with real consumer data and real regulators, run on free software?"
section: "How this works"
order: 10
---
Yes, and many do. The financial services industry runs an enormous amount of mission-critical infrastructure on open-source software. Banks run on Linux. Trading systems are built on PostgreSQL. The encryption that protects nearly all online financial transactions is implemented in open-source libraries. The notion that "free" implies "amateur" is a misconception left over from open source's earliest days.

What makes software trustworthy for regulated use is not whether you paid for it, but whether it is well-engineered, well-tested, well-documented, and well-maintained. The OpenAR Collective platform is being designed from the start with regulatory requirements in mind: cryptographically sealed audit trails, fine-grained access controls, configurable compliance workflows, and the kind of operational safeguards that regulators expect to see. The platform's compliance posture is a core design priority, not an afterthought.
